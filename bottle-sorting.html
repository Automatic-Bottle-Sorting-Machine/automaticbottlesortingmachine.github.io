PROJECT OVERVIEW

Turning Waste Into Data

01

The Problem

Manual waste separation can be slow, inconsistent and difficult to maintain in busy public environments. Incorrect sorting can also reduce the efficiency of recycling processes.

02

Our Approach

We developed a compact Arduino-based system that detects a bottle, identifies its material and mechanically directs it into the appropriate compartment.

03

Target Users

The prototype is designed as a low-cost educational and demonstration system suitable for institutions, offices and recycling-focused environments.

03

IMPLEMENTATION

From Detection to Sorting

01

Detect

The IR sensor detects a bottle entering the sorting chamber.

02

Check Metal

The inductive proximity sensor checks whether the bottle is metallic.

03

Measure Light

If the material is non-metal, the laser and LDR measure the transmitted light intensity.

04

Classify

A calibrated threshold separates high-intensity plastic readings from low-intensity paper readings.

05

Rotate

The 28BYJ-48 stepper motor rotates the platter toward the selected compartment.

06

Release

The SG90 servo opens the lid, releases the bottle, then closes the lid.

PROJECT REFINEMENT

What Changed

INTERIM REPORT II

A safer and more focused three-material system.

Following prototype evaluation, the system was redesigned around three practical categories: metal, plastic and paper. The optical sensing approach was introduced to improve non-metal classification.

+
Laser + LDR

Introduced for plastic and paper classification.

→
Three Compartments

Final prototype uses metal, plastic and paper.

✓
Safety Focus

Glass sorting was removed from the final scope.

ENGINEERING CHALLENGES

What We're Solving

01

Light Calibration

Finding a reliable LDR threshold is essential because surrounding light and material thickness can affect the sensor reading.

02

Mechanical Timing

Stepper movement and servo timing must be coordinated so the bottle is released correctly.

03

Material Testing

Multiple samples must be tested to verify that the classification threshold works consistently.
